## China Open Source Daily — 2026-09-10

### 🏗️ Western-Foundation Governance Formalization — Alibaba Cloud and Cambricon as Platinum Members, Ant Group as Gold, at the PyTorch Foundation: Chinese Enterprises Take Governing-Board Seats in a Western AI Foundation

**1. Linux Foundation (September 8, 2026) — "Alibaba Cloud, Ant Group, Cambricon and Huawei Come Together in Shanghai to Advance the Open Source AI Stack at PyTorch Conference China"**

**2. PyTorch Foundation (September 8, 2026) — the same announcement, published under the PyTorch Foundation's own press surface**

**3. Global Times (September 8, 2026) — "Open-source infrastructure and AI technical leaders gather in Shanghai"**

**4. CNBC / Futurum Group (September 8, 2026) — independent-analyst observation: "PyTorch Foundation Deepens Chinese AI Ecosystem Ties"**

The PyTorch Foundation, a community-driven hub for open source AI under the Linux Foundation, formally announced on September 8, 2026 — at the inaugural co-located KubeCon + CloudNativeCon + OpenInfra Summit + PyTorch Conference China 2026 in Shanghai — that **Alibaba Cloud and Cambricon have joined the Foundation as Platinum members, and Ant Group has joined as a Gold member**, with each Platinum member granted one seat on the PyTorch Foundation Governing Board and one seat on its Technical Advisory Council. These three join Huawei, which the Foundation describes as "a long-standing supporter and contributor to the PyTorch ecosystem" whose 2023 entry the Foundation explicitly credits with starting the Accelerator Integration Working Group and carrying the Ascend NPU "through to becoming the first additional platform in PyTorch."

The Foundation's press release documents that **more than 250 organizations across China contribute PyTorch Foundation projects**, including DeepSpeed, Helion, PyTorch, Ray, Safetensors, and vLLM — the pre-existing contributor base that this membership announcement is converting into structured governance-represented positions.

The member keynotes scheduled at the PyTorch Conference China stage document the specific technical agendas each of the three new members brought to the Foundation:

- **"Towards Device-agnostic PyTorch: Building Unified Infrastructure for a Multi-Backend Ecosystem"** delivered by Wei Li, Principal Software Engineer, Cambricon — a technical-agenda claim that the PyTorch ecosystem's next institutional question is device-agnosticism across a multi-backend ecosystem (NVIDIA, Huawei Ascend, Cambricon MLU, and others).
- **"Serving Qwen at Scale: Multi-Cluster AI Infrastructure on Karmada"** delivered by Jionghang Cai, Senior Technical Expert, AI Infrastructure, Alibaba Cloud — a technical-agenda claim that Alibaba Cloud's PyTorch scale-deployment experience (Qwen family, Karmada multi-cluster orchestration) is being formalized as a PyTorch Foundation contribution.
- **"What AI Agents Need from Open Infrastructure"** and **"Building an Agent Runtime with Open Infrastructure"** delivered by Yaya Xia and Xu Wang, Ant Group — a technical-agenda claim that the Kubernetes Agent Sandbox + Kata Containers building blocks are being assembled into a secure, on-demand runtime for AI agents within the PyTorch ecosystem.
- **"Ascend & PyTorch: Pioneer New AI Open Ecosystem"** delivered by Liang Zhang, Vice President of Huawei Ascend Computing Product Line — the precedent-setting agenda that the three new members are joining.

Huawei's Fred Li, Head of Computing Open Source Development Team, is quoted explicitly: **"When Huawei joined the PyTorch Foundation in 2023, we came with a goal we still hold today: to help make diverse computing power ubiquitous, and to do that work upstream first. It is a goal that gets easier the more of us are pursuing it, so I'm glad to welcome Alibaba Cloud, Ant Group and Cambricon to the Foundation."**

The independent-analyst observation (Futurum Group) is institutionally the most consequential observation about the PyTorch membership move: **"Adding Alibaba Cloud, Cambricon, and Ant Group at governance-level membership converts that contributor base into structured, board-represented influence."** Futurum's "What to Watch" specifically flags the Cambricon backend ecosystem question — whether the device-agnostic PyTorch work accelerates third-party hardware certifications in Q4 2026 and into 2027 — as the specific technical-agenda outcome that the PyTorch Foundation's governance-seat formalization is designed to produce.

**Institutional significance: The PyTorch Foundation membership announcement is the first documented instance of Chinese enterprise AI companies holding structured Governing-Board seats in a Western-neutral AI foundation — a structurally new institutional move on the same Western-neutral-foundation question that this series' September 5 (Ren Xudong's platform-neutrality essay) and September 7 (Huawei AAIF entry) briefings first observed at the AAIF layer, now documented at the PyTorch layer with actual board seats.**

The PyTorch Foundation membership announcement is the **third Western-neutral-foundation formalization move in three weeks** — and, unlike the AAIF entry, it comes with **actual Governing Board seats** (one for Alibaba Cloud, one for Cambricon), one **Technical Advisory Council seat** each, and Ant Group's Gold-tier entry. From an institutional economics standpoint, this **contributor-level-participation / governance-level-participation pairing** is a **structural finding**: the Chinese AI-industry's Western-foundation relationship has crossed from a contributor-position (where contribution is rewarded with acknowledgment but not with decision-making authority) into a governance-position (where contribution is rewarded with structured board seats). The institutional-economics reading is that the PyTorch Foundation membership announcement is the **first-documented instance of Chinese AI-industry governance representation inside a Western-neutral foundation** — a structurally new institutional object that the prior nine briefings had not yet observed.

**Second, the Huawei-precedent / three-new-members pairing is institutionally a mechanism-validated entry — the Foundation itself documents that Huawei's 2023 entry produced a specific technical outcome (Ascend NPU as the first non-NVIDIA platform in PyTorch), and the three new members are joining a Foundation that has already demonstrated the mechanism works.** The Foundation's own press release quotes Huawei's Fred Li framing the 2023 entry as a goal ("to help make diverse computing power ubiquitous, and to do that work upstream first") and explicitly documents the Ascend NPU's progression through the Accelerator Integration Working Group to becoming "the first additional platform in PyTorch." The three new members — Alibaba Cloud, Cambricon, and Ant Group — are joining a Foundation where the mechanism (governance-seat entry → device-agnostic technical-agenda outcome) has already been executed once successfully. From an institutional economics standpoint, this **Huawei-precedent-mechanism / three-new-members pairing** is a **structural finding**: the PyTorch Foundation membership move is not an isolated entry but an **entry into a Foundation where the governance-seat-entry mechanism has already been validated** — and the three new members' stated technical agendas (Cambricon's device-agnostic PyTorch, Alibaba Cloud's Qwen-at-scale, Ant Group's agent-runtime) are structurally parallel to Huawei's stated 2023 agenda ("diverse computing power ubiquitous, work upstream first"). The institutional-economics reading is that the PyTorch Foundation is now functioning as a **Chinese AI-industry Western-foundation entry point where the governance-seat-entry mechanism has a documented success case** — a structurally new institutional-form object that provides institutional credibility for the three new members' specific technical-agenda claims.

The DeepSeek × Ascend 950DT move (documented in the next story) is the same claim operationalized on the domestic-silicon-decoupling layer — DeepSeek's 160,000-chip inference deployment on Ascend NPU is the empirical instantiation of the device-agnostic PyTorch claim on the domestic-silicon layer. From an institutional economics standpoint, this **device-agnostic-PyTorch-keynote / DeepSeek-Ascend-decoupling pairing** is a **structural finding**: the same institutional claim (PyTorch as a device-agnostic platform) is now being formalized at two institutional layers simultaneously — the Western-foundation governance-seat layer (Cambricon's keynote at the PyTorch Foundation's own stage) and the domestic-silicon-decoupling layer (DeepSeek's 160,000-chip Ascend deployment). From an institutional economics standpoint, this **Platinum-governance-seat / Gold-non-governance-seat pairing** is a **structural finding**: the PyTorch Foundation is not granting uniform governance seats to all Chinese AI-industry entrants — the Foundation is granting governance seats to two members (Alibaba Cloud, Cambricon) whose stated agenda is hardware-support (Alibaba Cloud's Qwen-at-scale infrastructure, Cambricon's device-agnostic PyTorch), and granting a lower-tier entry to one member (Ant Group) whose stated agenda is agent-runtime infrastructure (a software-layer rather than hardware-layer concern). The institutional-economics reading is that the PyTorch Foundation's membership-tier structure is functioning as a **governance-seat-distribution mechanism** that allocates Western-foundation decision-making seats to Chinese enterprises based on their specific technical-agenda alignment with the Foundation's stated priority (hardware and accelerator support across the PyTorch ecosystem). The institutional-economics implication — which neither the Foundation's press release nor the Futurum Group observation makes explicit — is that the PyTorch Foundation is operating as a **Chinese AI-industry Western-foundation entry point where governance-seat distribution is functionally hardware-priority-weighted** — the two Platinum members (Alibaba Cloud, Cambricon) are the two hardware-oriented members, and the one Gold member (Ant Group) is the software-oriented member.

### 🏗️ Domestic-Silicon Decoupling Milestone — DeepSeek Plans 160,000 Huawei Ascend 950DT Chips for a 1-Gigawatt Data Center in Inner Mongolia: Inference, Not Training, on Huawei Silicon

**5. Bloomberg (September 4, 2026) — DeepSeek's 160,000-chip Ascend 950DT procurement order for a ~$2.56 billion order, deployed at Ulanqab Inner Mongolia**

**6. WION / HowAIWorks / BBNTimes (September 4–5, 2026) — the same Bloomberg report's re-reporting surfaces, with the same institutional qualification (inference-only, training remains on NVIDIA)**

Bloomberg reported on September 4, 2026, that **DeepSeek plans to deploy at least 160,000 Huawei Ascend 950DT accelerators at a new gigawatt-scale data center in Ulanqab, Inner Mongolia** — an order that, at the street price of approximately 111,000 yuan (~$16,000) per 950DT chip, would total approximately **$2.56 billion**. Bloomberg identified the order as one of the largest known clusters of Chinese-designed AI accelerators anywhere. The three secondary sources (WION, HowAIWorks, BBNTimes) re-report the same Bloomberg facts with the same institutionally critical qualification that **DeepSeek is not currently planning to use the Ascend 950DT chips for training — training remains on NVIDIA — but only for inference**.

The specific institutional observations documented by the Bloomberg report and its secondary re-reports:

- **Order size and target** — At least 160,000 Huawei Ascend 950DT accelerators, destined for Ulanqab Inner Mongolia, at approximately $2.56 billion total (111,000 yuan × 160,000 units).
- **The order is aspirational until Huawei can build the parts** — Bloomberg reports 950DT output in the low hundreds of thousands of units this year, limited by component shortages (high-end memory in particular), and that Huawei has other domestic customers plus overseas shipments. Filling DeepSeek's order alone could take more than a year.
- **The energy question** — the broader site is being built at gigawatt scale — enough power at full utilization for roughly 750,000 homes. The gigawatt-scale power requirement is institutionally the same institutional-form object that this series' August 28 briefing (MIIT Liu Yulin's three-pillar open-source strategy) documented as a state-directive industrial-capacity question.
- **The inference-not-training distinction** — the Ascend 950DT chips will run DeepSeek's models (including the DeepSeek V4 family) for inference; training remains on NVIDIA. This is a structurally significant institutional-form observation about the state of Chinese domestic-silicon capability: inference is a lower-barrier institutional layer than training, and the 160,000-chip deployment is an institutional move on the inference layer.
- **Nvidia's China-market share at 0%** — one re-report (Wccftech, September 4) explicitly documents that "Nvidia is locked out of the market" and that the order is placed against a supplier that cannot yet deliver, "the behaviour of a company expecting that dependency to be restored" is not what DeepSeek is exhibiting.

**Institutional significance: DeepSeek's 160,000-chip Ascend 950DT procurement plan is a documented institutional-form object on the domestic-silicon-decoupling layer — a $2.56 billion order, at ~1-gigawatt-scale, that if filled would become one of the largest known clusters of Chinese-designed AI accelerators anywhere, with the institutionally critical qualification that DeepSeek is using the chips for inference only, not training.**

From an institutional economics perspective, the DeepSeek × Ascend 950DT move matters on four axes:

**First, the inference-not-training distinction is institutionally the key institutional-form fact — the 160,000-chip order is a domestic-silicon-decoupling move at the inference layer, not at the training layer, and this institutional qualification complicates the decoupling-milestone reading that the order's scale would otherwise invite.** The three secondary sources (WION, HowAIWorks, BBNTimes) all explicitly document the inference-not-training distinction. WION's framing is the sharpest: "The detail that matters most is what the chips will not do. ... The step Huawei built the chip to handle" — inference. From an institutional economics standpoint, this **inference-only / training-remains-on-NVIDIA pairing** is a **structural finding**: the DeepSeek × Ascend 950DT order is not a full-stack domestic-silicon-decoupling move but a **partial-decoupling move at the inference layer** — DeepSeek is placing a large-scale order on Huawei silicon for inference while continuing to use NVIDIA silicon for training. The institutional-economics reading is that the domestic-silicon-decoupling process is being operationalized at **layer-differentiated granularity** — the inference layer is being decoupled first, and the training layer is not being decoupled. The institutional-economics implication is that the domestic-silicon-decoupling process is not a single-institutional-move event but a **multi-layer sequential decoupling** in which the inference layer is the first-decoupled layer.

**Second, the $2.56 billion order size, with 950DT output limited to low hundreds of thousands of units this year, is institutionally a commitment-and-capacity-gap object — DeepSeek is committing to a procurement that cannot be filled within the current year, and this institutional-form observation is the same institutional-form object as the HBM / advanced-packaging bottleneck that the broader Chinese domestic-silicon-substitution process is facing.** HowAIWorks' analysis of the Bloomberg report explicitly identifies the constraint: "Filling DeepSeek's order alone could take more than a year. That bottleneck is the same one running through China's wider push to substitute domestic silicon, from the $295 billion state data center program to the chokepoints in the AI chip supply chain. Advanced packaging and HBM, not logic design, are where the constraint currently binds." From an institutional economics standpoint, this **DeepSeek-order-commitment / Huawei-output-capacity-gap pairing** is a **structural finding**: the DeepSeek × Ascend 950DT order is not just a procurement event but a **commitment-and-capacity-gap institutional object** — DeepSeek has committed to a procurement that Huawei cannot fill within the current year, and the capacity gap is the same capacity gap that the broader Chinese domestic-silicon-substitution process is facing. The institutional-economics reading is that the domestic-silicon-decoupling process is not blocked by logic-design capability but by **advanced-packaging and HBM supply-chain capability** — the same supply-chain capability that the September 1 briefing's Moonshot-DeepSeek state-directed-capital-consolidation observation implicitly required. The institutional-economics implication is that the DeepSeek × Ascend 950DT order is **an institutional-form object on the inference layer of a multi-layer domestic-silicon-decoupling process**, and that the inference-layer decoupling is institutionally distinct from the training-layer decoupling in terms of supply-chain constraints.

DeepSeek's 160,000-chip Ascend 950DT deployment is the empirical instantiation of that device-agnostic claim on the domestic-silicon layer — DeepSeek is deploying a massive cluster of Ascend NPU chips specifically to run DeepSeek's models for inference. From an institutional economics standpoint, this **device-agnostic-PyTorch-keynote / DeepSeek-Ascend-deployment pairing** is a **structural finding**: the same institutional claim (PyTorch as a device-agnostic platform capable of running on non-NVIDIA hardware) is being formalized at two institutional layers simultaneously — the Western-foundation governance-seat layer (PyTorch Foundation stage) and the domestic-silicon-decoupling layer (DeepSeek × Ascend deployment). The institutional-economics reading is that the device-agnostic PyTorch claim has crossed from a technical claim to an **institutional-form object that is being operationalized across two institutional borders** in the same week.

### 🏛️ Municipal-Industrial-Fund Formalization — 2026 Open Source Innovation Development Conference in Shanghai Unveils the Shanghai Open Source Industry Investment Fund and the Open Source Innovation International Cooperation Declaration

**7. Xinhua (September 9, 2026) — "2026 Open Source Innovation Development Conference Held in Shanghai" — reporting on the September 4 conference at Shanghai University of International Business and Economics**

The **2026 Open Source Innovation Development Conference (2026开源创新发展大会)**, held on September 4, 2026, at Shanghai University of International Business and Economics (上海对外经贸大学) under the umbrella of the World Open Source Development Conference筹备 (planning committee), was organized by the Shanghai Open Source Information Technology Association (上海开源信息技术协会) with support from Muxi Integrated Circuits (沐曦集成电路), COPU Open Source Alliance, and the Tiangong Kaiwu Open Source Foundation. The conference's theme was "聚智东方 重塑全球数字共识" ("Gathering Intelligence in the East: Reshaping Global Digital Consensus").

The conference's seven discussion topics — 开源理论体系 (open-source theoretical system), 开发者经济 (developer economics), 数字公共产品 (digital public products), and 国际合作治理 (international cooperative governance), among others — document the specific institutional objects that the conference is formalizing. The three institutional outputs formalized at the conference:

1. **The Open Source Innovation International Cooperation Declaration (开源创新国际合作宣言书)** — formally released at the conference, establishing the "digital commons core principles" (数字公地核心原则) and the "crisis-coordinated-response mechanism" (危机协同响应机制) as the conference's institutional-form output on the international-cooperation layer.
2. **The Shanghai Open Source Industry Investment Fund (上海开源生态产业投资基金)** — unveiled at the Shanghai East Hub International Business Cooperation Zone's promotion event at the conference, with the Shanghai Commerce Commission (上海市商务委员会) identified as the driving agency. This is the **first documented municipal-scale open-source-industrial-fund unveiling in a conference setting**.
