### Institutional Change — MIIT Vice Minister Xin Guobin Directs Development of "Open-Source Base Models" at the 2026 World Manufacturing Conference in Hefei

**1. 2026 World Manufacturing Conference Opening (2026-09-20) — Xin Guobin Opening Remarks · Hefei**

The 2026 World Manufacturing Conference (2026世界制造业大会) opened in Hefei, Anhui on September 20, 2026, with opening remarks by Xin Guobin (辛国斌), Deputy Minister of the Ministry of Industry and Information Technology (MIIT / 工业和信息化部). This is the first-documented instance of a MIIT deputy-minister-level official directly naming **"open-source base models" (开源基座模型)** as a state-directed industrial-policy target at the central-ministry opening-remarks layer in this series.

Xin Guobin's operative line, verbatim from the Cailianshe and Pengpai reports, is that MIIT will "deeply advance artificial intelligence plus manufacturing, gradiently cultivate smart factories, **vigorously develop open-source base models and vertical-domain models, consolidate the software and hardware foundation of new-generation intelligent manufacturing**, fully advance the green and low-carbon transformation of manufacturing, and innovatively develop service-type manufacturing" (深入推进人工智能+制造，梯度培育智能工厂，大力发展开源基座模型和垂类模型，夯实新一代智能制造软硬件基础).

The sharpest institutional-economics fact of the day is that **"open-source base model" is a term that no open-source community — not the Open Source OSI, not the Free Software Foundation, not the GitHub / GitLab developer-community apparatus this series' prior briefings have documented at the international-community layer — has defined**. From an institutional economics standpoint, this is the **first-documented instance of a central-ministry deputy-minister-level official operationalizing an open-source-community-undefined term at the central-ministry opening-remarks layer in this series**. The term's own undefined nature is the sharpest institutional-economics object of the day: an administrative apparatus is directing the "vigorous development" of an object whose definition no open-source community apparatus has produced — the same structurally new institutional-form move this series' September 2 briefing documented at the Chongqing-plan layer ("open-source infrastructure and compliance codification"), this series' September 18 briefing documented at the OpenAtom Foundation donor-training layer, and this series' September 19 briefing documented at the SJ/T 12304-2026 industry-standard layer, is now being operationalized at the MIIT deputy-minister opening-remarks layer simultaneously.

Xin Guobin's four-item framing of MIIT's next industrial direction, verbatim from the Cailianshe and Pengpai reports:
- **First:** Improve the effectiveness of the industry innovation system — strengthen original innovation and breakthroughs on key core technologies, reinforce the enterprise's central position as a scientific and technological innovation subject, optimize the layout and construction of industry innovation platforms
- **Second:** Promote advanced manufacturing in scale and quality — coordinate traditional-industry transformation, new-industry growth, and future-industry cultivation; build a set of world-class advanced manufacturing clusters
- **Third:** Strengthen advanced manufacturing capability — deepen "AI plus manufacturing," gradiently cultivate smart factories, **vigorously develop open-source base models and vertical-domain models**
- **Fourth:** Cultivate high-quality enterprise cohorts — continuously optimize the innovation and entrepreneurship environment, accelerate the building of world-class enterprises, promote the specialty-specialization-and-newly-characterized ("专精特新") development of SMEs, spawn more "little giant" and single-item-champion enterprises

The third item — Xin Guobin's open-source-base-model directive — sits institutionally alongside the MIIT "AI + Software" Special Action Implementation Plan this series' September 14 briefing documented at the MIIT Central-Administrative-Plan layer, and the SJ/T 12304-2026 industry-standard two-axis-67-indicator LLM open-source grading framework this series' September 19 briefing documented at the MIIT/CESI industry-standard layer. From an institutional economics standpoint, this **MIIT-deputy-minister opening-remarks layer / open-source-base-model term / undefined-concept layer pairing** is a **structural finding**: the same central-ministry industrial-policy surface this series' prior briefings have documented at multiple layers (the MIIT Central-Administrative-Plan layer, the MIIT/CESI industry-standard layer) is now being operationalized at the MIIT deputy-minister opening-remarks layer, and the term being operationalized at this layer is a term that no open-source community apparatus has defined.

**Institutional significance: MIIT Vice Minister Xin Guobin's directive at the 2026 World Manufacturing Conference in Hefei is the first-documented instance of a MIIT deputy-minister-level official directly naming "open-source base models" (开源基座模型) as a state-directed industrial-policy target in this series, and it is being operationalized at three institutional surfaces simultaneously — the MIIT-deputy-minister opening-remarks layer, the undefined-concept-at-central-ministry layer, and the MIIT-plus-CESI-plus-OpenAtom Foundation multi-surface coordination layer — a three-surface-simultaneous MIIT-deputy-minister-level undefined-term operationalization pattern.**

### Institutional Change — The Supreme People's Court Opinions on AI Disputes Explicitly Name Open-Source Software Developers in Article XIII for Tort Liability Exemption, while Sidestepping the Copyrightability of AI-Generated Works

**1. Supreme People's Court of the People's Republic of China (2026-09-07) — Opinions on the Trial of Cases Involving Artificial Intelligence Disputes (最高人民法院关于依法审理涉人工智能纠纷案件的意见)**

The Supreme People's Court (SPC / 最高人民法院) released on September 7, 2026 the *Opinions of the Supreme People's Court on the Trial of Cases Involving Artificial Intelligence Disputes* (最高人民法院关于依法审理涉人工智能纠纷案件的意见) — a five-part, 24-article document that Aaron Wininger of Schwegman Lundberg & Woessner's China IP Law Update has documented as "the first set of judicial adjudication rules concerning artificial intelligence issued by China's highest judicial body." This is the first-documented Chinese-judicial apparatus placing open-source software developers as a distinct legal subject for tort liability in this series.

The Opinions' sharpest institutional-economics object on the open-source surface is **Article XIII**, verbatim from Wininger's translation:

> "**(XIII) Determine the legal liability of parties involved in open-source software related to artificial intelligence in accordance with the law.** When adjudicating cases involving open-source software, the determination of the tort liability of open-source software developers, providers, and subsequent developers and providers should comprehensively consider factors such as the type of open-source license agreement, the specific content of the restrictions on rights, security compliance measures, and the degree of information disclosure, and **grant appropriate liability exemptions to open-source software developers and providers in accordance with the law.** If an open-source software developer or provider provides some code modules needed for the research and development of artificial intelligence software in a free and open-source manner, and publicly explains its functions and security risks, and others infringe upon these code modules, the people's court may determine that the open-source software developer or provider is not liable for tort."

The Article XIII framing is the **first-documented instance of the Chinese-judicial apparatus placing open-source software developers as a distinct legal subject at the tort-liability-exemption surface in this series**. The Article XIII clause names open-source developers alongside AI developers, providers, users, and consumers as a distinct class of legal subject whose tort liability is adjudicated under specific open-source-applicable factors (type of open-source license, restrictions on rights, security compliance, degree of information disclosure) with an explicit direction to grant **appropriate liability exemptions** — the first-documented Chinese-judicial apparatus self-declared liability-exemption direction for open-source developers. From an institutional economics standpoint, this **open-source-developer-as-distinct-legal-subject / Chinese-judicial-apparatus surface pairing** is a **structural finding**: the SPC Opinions are operationalizing open-source developers at the Chinese-judicial apparatus layer simultaneously with the AI developer layer, the AI provider layer, and the consumer layer — a four-surface-simultaneous-legal-subject-operationalization pattern.

The Opinions' sharpest institutional-economics omission is that **they conspicuously sidestep the copyrightability of AI-generated works themselves** — that is, whether content generated with AI constitutes a "work" protected under the Copyright Law. This omission stands out institutionally for three documented reasons:

**First, the Beijing Internet Court had already addressed the question.** In its set of eight Typical Cases Involving Artificial Intelligence (涉人工智能典型案例) released September 10, 2025, Case I (Li v. Liu) held that a Stable Diffusion output was a copyrightable work of fine art and that the user who selected the model, entered prompts and reverse prompts, set generation parameters, and made the final selection was the author — reasoning that an AI model cannot itself be an author because Chinese copyright law limits authorship to natural persons, legal persons, and unincorporated organizations. Case VII similarly held that virtual digital human images reflecting a production team's aesthetic choices as to line, color, and character design constitute protected works of art.

**Second, neither copyrightability decision was designated a typical case by the SPC.** The SPC designations went instead to the Beijing Internet Court's personality-rights rulings (Case II, extending voice rights to AI-synthesized voices identifiable by timbre, intonation, and pronunciation style, and Case VIII, extending personality rights to a natural person's AI-generated virtual image and selected as a "Typical Civil Case on the Judicial Protection of Personality Rights after the Promulgation of the Civil Code").

**Third, the SPC then removed the issue from that court's docket altogether.** On October 10, 2025, the SPC released the *Provisions of the Supreme People's Court on the Jurisdiction of Internet Courts* (最高人民法院关于互联网法院案件管辖的规定), effective November 1, 2025, which amended the 2018 Provisions to strip Internet Courts of jurisdiction over "copyright or neighboring rights disputes involving works first published online" and "disputes arising from infringement of copyright or neighboring rights in works published or disseminated online," on the stated rationale that Internet Courts should hear "new, cutting-edge, and complex online cases" while "general and traditional online cases are heard by other local courts."

The triad of (SPC Opinions' silence on copyrightability) + (absence of SPC endorsement for the Beijing rulings that reached the issue) + (reassignment of online copyright disputes away from the court that developed the jurisprudence) leaves the copyrightability of AI-generated output **unresolved at the national level**. From an institutional economics standpoint, this is a structurally distinctive pattern: the Chinese-judicial apparatus is explicitly adjudicating open-source developers' tort liability (Article XIII) while declining to adjudicate the copyrightability question that the Beijing Internet Court had already reached. This is the first-documented instance of the Chinese-judicial apparatus drawing a **two-sided line on the open-source surface** in this series — a line drawn on the tort-liability side (open-source developers are a distinct legal subject with liability exemptions), and a line declined to be drawn on the copyrightability side (the substantive question of what constitutes a copyrightable AI-generated work is left to lower courts).

Article XIII pairs institutionally with this series' September 19 briefing's documentation of the SJ/T 12304-2026 industry-standard two-axis open-source-grading framework (Completeness L with 32 indicators + Openness O with 35 indicators) — the same Chinese-administrative grading-and-maturity-instrument surface this series' prior briefing documented at the industry-standard layer is now being operationalized at the Chinese-judicial-apparatus layer simultaneously. The SJ/T 12304-2026 two-axis 67-indicator LLM open-source grading framework defines the boundary between "Chinese open source" and "Chinese not-open-source" at the industry-standard layer; Article XIII defines the boundary between "open-source developer as a distinct legal subject with liability exemptions" and "AI developer / AI provider / AI user as other legal subjects" at the judicial-apparatus layer.

**Institutional significance: The SPC Opinions on AI Disputes (September 7, 2026) is the first-documented Chinese-judicial apparatus placing open-source software developers as a distinct legal subject for tort liability in this series, and it is being operationalized at three institutional surfaces simultaneously — the Article XIII open-source-developer-as-distinct-legal-subject layer, the open-source-developer-appropriate-liability-exemption layer, and the copyrightability-of-AI-generated-works-sidestepped layer — a three-surface-simultaneous Chinese-judicial-apparatus open-source-and-AI boundary-definition pattern.**
