Susan Finder's September 29, 2026 essay on the Supreme People's Court Monitor is the **first-documented international-mass-media exposure of the SPC's AI-opinion drafting process** in this series — a first-documented SPC-drafting-process-and-open-source-article-xiii-authorship layer for this series' September 21 briefing's SPC-Article-XIII-open-source-developer-liability-exemption layer.

**The sharpest institutional-economics fact of the day: the SPC Research Office drafting group's composition, its institutional origin in Xi Jinping's June 2026 guidance plus the 15th Five-Year Plan's AI-governance language, and its closed-consultation-with-industry-but-not-public drafting procedure.** Per Finder: "The SPC established a drafting group for the AI Policy Document in 2025, led by several senior staff in the SPC's Research Office, with a major contribution from the #3 Civil Division, which focuses on intellectual property." Per Finder: "The political reasons are anchored by guidance from Xi Jinping... 'The more rapidly [artificial intelligence] technology develops, the more correctly we must anchor it in the direction of being positive, benevolent, and beneficial to mankind; the more precisely we must grasp the scale of supervision and governance; and the more promptly we must improve measures to prevent it from getting out of control.'" Per Finder: "The Outline of the 15th Five-Year Plan (国民经济和社会发展第十五个五年规划纲要) states that it is necessary to 'improve the laws, regulations, policies, application standards, and ethical guidelines in the field of artificial intelligence.'" Per Finder: "The drafters first engaged in desk research and then sought views from local courts, relevant government institutions, industry, and selected experts and scholars, but not the general public."

**The Article XIII as the open-source-relevant surface of this drafting apparatus.** Per Hogan Lovells' September 18 analysis: "Article 13 carves out a conditional exemption for open-source contributors. Where a developer freely releases code modules used in AI software and publishes clear documentation of the modules' functions and safety risks, the courts have the discretion to find no liability for downstream infringement caused by others using those modules. The statutory language is deliberately permissive, 'may' rather than 'shall', so that judges have room to hold an open-source provider accountable where the facts justify it. The courts can also weigh the licence type, any restrictions on use, the provider's own compliance posture, and the quality of its risk disclosures." The Article XIII clause names open-source developers alongside AI developers, providers, users, and consumers as a distinct class of legal subject whose tort liability is adjudicated under specific open-source-applicable factors (type of open-source license, restrictions on rights, security compliance, degree of information disclosure) with an explicit direction to grant **appropriate liability exemptions**.

**The institutional-object pairing with this series' September 21 briefing's SPC-Article-XIII-open-source-developer-liability-exemption-plus-copyrightability-of-AI-generated-works-sidestepped layer.** This series' September 21 briefing documented the Article XIII clause verbatim and the copyrightability-silence pattern at the legal-institute analysis layer. The institutional-economics significance is that this **Article-XIII-authorship-plus-closed-consultation pairing** documents the specific institutional actors that produced the open-source-liability-exemption clause and the specific consultation procedure that produced it, at the same layer where this series' September 21 briefing documented the clause's substantive content.

**The institutional-object pairing with this series' September 3 briefing's CAC-naming-of-Doubao-Qwen-WenxinYiyan-and-August-31-Supreme-People's-Court-roundtable layer.** This series' September 3 briefing documented the CAC Qinglang Phase 2 announcement that named specific AI platform subjects for enforcement — the first-documented tiered-apparatus-moving-from-policy-in-formation-to-named-subjects-enforcement action. **The institutional-object pairing with this series' September 21 briefing's SPC-Opinions-sidesteps-copyrightability-of-AI-generated-works-plus-Jones-Day-September-2026-legal-analysis layer.** This series' September 21 briefing documented that the SPC Opinions "conspicuously sidestep the copyrightability of AI-generated works themselves" and that the SPC then removed the issue from the Beijing Internet Court's docket. Per Hogan Lovells: "The Opinion does not address the hotly contested issue of the copyrightability of AI-generated content and whether using copyrighted works to train AI models is infringing." Per Hogan Lovells: "It has been reported that during the drafting process, views on both issues diverged sharply, and the SPC concluded that further case-by-case experience was needed before committing to a hard rule." The institutional-economics significance is that the closed-consultation-with-industry-but-not-public procedure, and the drafting-group's composition (Research Office plus #3 Civil Division), produced both the Article XIII open-source-liability-exemption clause and the copyrightability-silence — two institutionally divergent decisions at the same drafting apparatus.

**The institutional-economics question this cycle's briefing documents but does not answer.** Per Finder: "The drafters first engaged in desk research and then sought views from local courts, relevant government institutions, industry, and selected experts and scholars, but not the general public." The institutional-economics question is whether the closed-consultation-with-industry-but-not-public procedure converts the SPC-Article-XIII-open-source-developer-liability-exemption clause into an industry-consulted-object rather than an open-community-consulted-object, and whether the same drafting apparatus's silence on the copyrightability of AI-generated works plus the silence on the legality of training on copyrighted works converts the SPC-Opinions-surface into a two-sided-silence-object rather than a rules-carried-by-laws-object.
