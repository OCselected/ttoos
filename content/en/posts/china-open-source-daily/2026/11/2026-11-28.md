⚠️ **Editorial note: The open source ecosystem in China operates under a distinct institutional framework — characterized by state-led initiatives, intranet-like boundaries, and top-down governance. Readers should be aware that this context differs from the community-driven open source model common in other regions. The term "open source" used in Chinese media may refer to practices that diverge from the conventional definition.**

## China Open Source Daily — 2026-11-28

### 🏗️ Institutional Change — The Receipt and the Radius: What a Fintech Bought When It Bought the Weights

**1. The case, with the numbers that were stated at a conference and not in an announcement.** On **2026-11-03**, **The Register** reported the details of a talk given by **David Abitbol**, who runs AI operations and infrastructure at **Revolut**, at the **AI Engineer** conference in New York the previous month[^1]. Revolut moved approximately **2,000 engineers** from **Anthropic's Claude Code** and **OpenAI's Codex** onto a **self-hosted Chinese open-weight model** — **Z.ai's GLM**, first **GLM-5.1** and then **GLM-5.3** — served on **56 Nvidia B200 GPUs** the company deployed itself. The reported results and the reported constraints, kept separate because the coverage blurred them:

- **Inference cost fell by roughly 90 per cent**, to about **USD 0.30 per million input/output tokens**, described as roughly a tenth of Anthropic's or OpenAI's rates.
- **Coding time improved 60 per cent; lead time improved 70 per cent.**
- **GLM-5.1 is not small**: roughly **754 billion total parameters with 40 billion activated per token**, needing about **380 GB of GPU memory** for the weights alone and a further **6–26 GB per user** of context depending on precision.
- The engineering work that made it viable was **not model work**: hyperparameter tuning, **NVFP4 quantisation**, disaggregated prefill and decode, and context-length tuning — reaching **sub-3-second time to first token at 256,000 tokens** and about **200 tokens per second** of generation, with engineers signing in after 5 pm seeing closer to 800[^1].
- **It did not replace the frontier.** GLM is text-only, so a **model router** still sends vision queries to **Gemini or GPT Luna**; code generation runs on GLM[^1].
- **Abitbol's own two cautions**: *"Long context is a scam… even though you can serve one million tokens, 256 is really what you need"*; and on the security objection, that the concern attaches to **calling a model through an API running out of China**, and that self-hosted or regionally hosted weights are a different proposition[^1].
- The economics he described are the point of the whole story: *"Open source is now a priority at Revolut, and our economics are favorable. The billing is flat, we don't have any bill shock at the end of the month, and the frontier is the exception."*[^1]

**2. What the pre-switch regime cost, because that is where the governance lesson lives.** Revolut had deployed **Gemini in early 2025** and **Claude Code in early 2026**. Abitbol's account: *"We were highly skeptical about the cost of AI. We were seeing engineers spend USD 1,000 a month, USD 10,000 a month with not so much ROI, and so when we launched it we launched it with strict budget limits."* The consequence was rationing — engineers **saved limited, expensive tokens for the most difficult tasks**, and hoarded them toward quarter-end[^1]. **When tokens became flat-cost, engineers stopped conserving them, and the largest reported gains came not from coding but from debugging and from Jira and Confluence tickets**: *"When you have unlimited tokens, you're free to do that."*[^1]

**3. The same case travelled through Chinese state media first, and that is how it entered this series' record.** On **2026-11-12**, **Xinhua's** Lisbon despatch from the **Web Summit** carried the case as evidence for a national claim: Revolut had replaced OpenAI and Anthropic coding assistants with **Zhipu's (智谱) open-source GLM series**, with GLM described as the default coding model for its roughly 2,000 engineers, coding time down about **60 per cent** and AI call cost down near **90 per cent**[^2]. The despatch is the sourced basis for what this series noted on **2026-11-16** and **2026-11-20**. **What is new today is not the story. It is that a trade-press account has now published the procurement detail behind it** — the GPU count, the quantisation, the per-token cost, the context decision, the router that keeps a frontier dependency — and that the detail reframes the case from a logo win into a **build-versus-buy decision with an itemised bill**[^1].

**4. What was actually bought, stated precisely.** Revolut did not buy a licence. **A permissive licence costs nothing and commits the vendor to nothing.** Revolut bought **56 B200 GPUs**, a serving stack, and the **right to keep serving the model after the vendor changes its mind** — and the thing it gave up was the vendor's roadmap and the vendor's frontier capabilities. **That is the only structural promise an open-weight release makes, and it is the only one that survived contact with a regulated financial institution.**

---

**Institutional Analysis & Commentary:**

**This is the first complete receipt this series has seen for what a Chinese open-weight licence is worth, and the receipt was issued by the buyer rather than the seller — which is exactly what makes it admissible.**

**First, name the transaction in Williamson's terms, because the case is a textbook governance choice and nobody in the coverage framed it that way.** Revolut's problem in early 2026 was not model quality; it was that **it could not predict its own marginal cost of an input its engineers were consuming at an accelerating rate**, and it responded with budget caps, which changed engineer behaviour toward hoarding[^1]. **That is what a metered commons does to its users: it teaches them to conserve an input whose marginal cost, from the firm's point of view, is a governance hazard rather than a signal.** Self-hosting converted a **variable, vendor-priced, unforecastable cost** into a **capital expenditure with a flat marginal cost** — and the behavioural change is the part no procurement department could have specified or bought. **Asset specificity was inverted: rather than the buyer becoming dependent on a specific vendor's terms, the buyer acquired a specific asset it owns and made the vendor's terms irrelevant.** Few moves in this whole subject produce that shift, and none of the instruments this series has catalogued in November produces it at all.

**Second, the 90 per cent is real and it is narrower than it looks, and the honesty is the part worth crediting.** Revolut still routes vision queries to Gemini or GPT Luna[^1], so this is not a substitute for a frontier lab — it is a **substitute for the commodity half of the frontier labs' business**, and Abitbol's phrase for the remainder is exact: *"the frontier is the exception."* **The model a regulated bank self-hosts is the model that does the routine work**, so the case measures the price of the commodity tier of intelligence rather than the price of capability. Read against **Menlo Ventures'** finding in this series' **2026-11-20** entry — open weights at **19 per cent of tokens and 4 per cent of spend**, open-source tokens averaging **USD 0.13 per million against a blended closed price of USD 0.70** — Revolut's **USD 0.30 all-in for its own serving sits above the market's average realised open-source price** while serving a workload it controls end to end. **Self-hosting on 56 B200 GPUs is not the cheap option; it is the predictable option.** For a bank, predictability is worth more than the last few cents, and that preference is exactly what a licence cannot charge for.

**Third, and this is the finding with no counterpart anywhere in this series' Chinese coverage: the security objection was answered by deployment architecture, not by a policy.** Abitbol's formulation — the concern attaches to **API calls routed through China**, while self-hosted or regionally hosted weights are a different proposition — is the practical resolution of a problem that has occupied European and American procurement offices for a year. **A weight file that arrives once and runs on your own GPUs cannot be switched off, cannot be repriced, and cannot be quietly altered.** That is what a download purchases, and it is a **governance property rather than a technical one**: exit options over the asset belong to whoever holds the copy. **The corollary, which the producing side of this transaction has not internalised: every self-hoster is a decision the vendor cannot recall.** A price that cannot be raised is a governance right that has already been transferred, once and for all, at the moment of the download.

**Fourth, the uncomfortable comparison with the domestic record, and it is why the case belongs in a briefing about China rather than about Britain.** This series has spent November documenting **instruments that govern an artefact's passage and not a contributor's capacity**: quality tiers, donation ladders, contribution counts, certification regimes, export registration. **The Revolut case contains none of those and one thing all of them lack — a buyer who could walk away with the artefact and prove the result to his own board.** **The strongest evidence for the value of Chinese open weights in this entire series was produced by a British fintech's infrastructure lead, at a New York conference, in a talk about GPU tuning** — not by any foundation, ministry or academy in the ecosystem that produces the weights. **That is not a criticism of the weights. It is the same structural observation this series made on 2026-11-27 about who reads the filings: the ecosystem publishes, and somebody outside it does the reading.** With models, the reading is a serving benchmark, and it is published by the party paying the bill.

**Fifth, the falsifiable tests, all observable.**

**(i) Does Revolut or a comparable buyer publish the total cost of ownership — GPUs, depreciation, power, staff — against the 90 per cent inference saving?** The talk reported inference cost, not TCO, and 56 B200s are not a rounding error. A capex substitution can win on the marginal line and lose on the fully loaded one.

**(ii) Does a second regulated institution in a Western jurisdiction repeat the move, and does it name the licence version?** One fintech is a datapoint; two regulated banks is a pattern. The licence version is what tells you whether the freedom was real, because **a permissive licence today is a promise about yesterday's file and nothing about tomorrow's.**

**(iii) Does Z.ai restrict self-hosting, or change GLM's licence, in a subsequent release?** This series' **2026-11-22** entry documented DeepSeek's shareholders holding economic rights and no votes and asked what the first post-IPO licence would be worth. **The Revolut case shows what defection would cost: a self-hoster is not a hostage, and a vendor that discovers this responds by pricing the next release differently — or by not releasing it.**

### 📊 The Ledger — The Same Figure Eight Times: What a Discretionary Number Cannot Be

**1. The observation, stated as narrowly as the evidence allows.** This briefing has been unable to reconcile a run of revenue figures published by Chinese AI sellers over the past month. **The pattern is not that the numbers are wrong — this run has no evidence any of them is — but that they cannot be checked**, and the reason is structural rather than adversarial:

- **Segment categories in the press are not statutory categories in the filings.** "AI business income", "agent platform income", "MaaS revenue" and "intelligent-computing revenue" are marketing segments; the statutory statement aggregates them differently, so a quoted figure cannot be tied to a line by subtraction without the company's help.
- **The same numeral recurs across unrelated sellers and unrelated quantities.** The number **108** has appeared in this fortnight both as an **absolute count — OpenAtom's 108 corporate donors in 2025, up from 47 in 2024**, analysed in this series on **2026-11-27**[^3] — and as a **revenue descriptor** applied by several sellers to unrelated reporting periods. **Two different quantities, one numeral, in a press ecosystem that does not distinguish them** is the observational fact on the table. Nothing here suggests anyone copied anything; **the numeral collides because it is memorable, not because it is shared.**
- **Where disclosure is discretionary, successive versions do not supersede each other in the press.** DeepSeek's second round moved from an original **CNY 50 billion** target to a final **CNY 105.5 billion** at a **CNY 615.5 billion** post-money valuation across roughly **31** external partners, and all versions were published as news without any report characterising itself as the correction of the last[^4].

**2. What is published prominently, and what is published only under compulsion.** Growth rates, market shares and segment totals appear in marketing and in state-media reporting. **Per-segment revenue, cost of revenue and gross margin appear only where a listing rule or an audit obligation compels them**, and then in the statutory key. **The gap between those two disclosure surfaces is the whole story**, and every instrument this series has catalogued in November lives on the first surface — a maturity grade, a donation tier, a contribution count, a certification — none of which is a quantity that can be falsified by subtraction.

---

**Institutional Analysis & Commentary:**

**Akerlof's 1970 paper is remembered for lemons and it argued something harder: dishonesty damages the honest seller too. The second half of that argument is the one that bears on disclosure regimes, and it has a corollary this ecosystem has not priced.**

**First, restate Akerlof's taxonomy as he stated it, because the third cost is the one nobody quotes.** The costs of dishonesty, in his 1970 *The Market for "Lemons"*: **(1)** the loss suffered by buyers of bad goods; **(2)** the loss suffered by sellers of good goods who must compete with bad — the externality; and **(3)** *"the cost of dishonesty… is the loss of the business of the sellers of good goods"*, because the presence of bad goods drives the price down and makes an honest claim unobtainable at a fair price[^5]. **Applied to discretionary disclosure: where a segment figure cannot be checked, a company whose true number is larger than the pattern is punished for accuracy, because a larger true figure reads as inflation.** **The rational response is not to publish more; it is to publish the figure that is most legible** — and the observable consequence is convergence in narrative across competitors rather than divergence, because divergence is what attracts scrutiny. **This is an inference from the published pattern and is labelled as such; it is a claim about incentives, not about anyone's conduct.**

**Second, the signalling problem is structural, and the ecosystem's answer to it has been certification rather than arithmetic.** In a lemons market quality is verified by **inspection, third-party certification, or a bond posted by the seller**. China has built the certification apparatus at scale — CAICT's trusted-open-source assessments, the SOSS national-standard test, SCA tooling evaluations, and the AI-supply-chain extensions this series covered on **2026-11-11** and **2026-11-19** — and **every one of those instruments certifies a governance process rather than a published quantity**. **A maturity grade tells a buyer that a supplier has a management system; it tells him nothing about whether the headline figure is a segment total, a run-rate, an annualised quarter, or a booking.** OpenAtom's audited series is the exception precisely because it is arithmetic that **reconciles across a year boundary** — opening net assets of **CNY 162,185,865.27** in the 2025 report equalling the end-2024 close, as this series verified on **2026-11-27**. **A filing is a stronger disclosure instrument than a quality award, and the reason is that a filing can be falsified by subtraction.**

**Third, the open-weight release is the genuine escape from the lemons problem, and that is the connection to today's first entry.** Revolut could verify **everything it needed to know about GLM before signing a contract**: the weights, the architecture, the serving cost, the throughput, the licence. **Not one of those facts required trusting a Chinese company's press release.** It ran the model on its own GPUs and measured. **That is the only procurement in this briefing where the buyer's evidence is independent of the seller's narrative** — and it is why the case deserved a full entry rather than a footnote. **Open weights are China's one product in this ecosystem whose quality a stranger can verify himself, and their global adoption is proceeding on exactly that basis**: DeepSeek's **15 per cent** and Kimi's **61 per cent** of enterprise open-source spend, per Menlo, are shares of a market where **the buyer checked**.

**Fourth, the symmetry that should worry the domestic programme, stated as a question about incentives rather than about honesty.** The instruments catalogued in November all **measure the seller's own inputs**: donations given, projects donated, developers thanked, installs counted, licences certified. **None measures an output a third party verified.** Revolut's receipt exists because a **buyer** with a budget had an incentive to publish. **In a domestic market where the buyer is frequently a state customer, a subsidised adopter, or a provincial platform, the buyer has no incentive to publish the bill and often no independent ability to compute it.** **The verifier is the party whose interest the subsidy removes.** That is the structural finding, and it explains why the strongest measurement of Chinese AI published this month came from a British bank in a New York conference hall rather than from inside the ecosystem that built the models.

**Fifth, the checkable questions, every one answerable from a document.**

**(i) For each seller quoting a segment figure, what is the corresponding statutory line, and does the arithmetic reconcile?** A prospectus with two successive periods makes this computable in one afternoon by anyone who owes the company nothing.

**(ii) Does any Chinese AI company publish per-segment gross margin alongside segment revenue?** Revenue without margin is the figure most compatible with narrative disclosure and least useful to a buyer.

**(iii) Does CAICT's trusted-assessment regime add a financial-disclosure dimension, on the model of the audit reconciliation OpenAtom now publishes?** The instrument exists; the dimension does not. **A maturity grade and an audited income statement are both about trustworthiness, and only one of them can be checked by subtraction.**

### 🔍 Commentary — Two Receipts, One Publishable

**First: the week's genuine finding is that the two most credible measurements of Chinese AI published this month came from outside the ecosystem, and for the same reason.** Revolut measured the model by running it. The Europe–China Digital Commons Observatory measured the foundation by OCR'ing its filings. **In both cases the measuring party had an incentive the seller could not supply: a budget to defend and a reputation staked on the correction.** The weight file made the first possible; a scanned PDF on a public URL made the second possible; **neither required the seller's permission, and that is the entire design.**

**Second: open weights are the one Chinese institutional export in this sector that is genuinely a commons, and the contrast with the domestic instruments is not about quality.** Consider what a stranger can do with each thing this ecosystem has put into the world. **With a weight file: download it, run it, benchmark it, ship a product on it, and never speak to the vendor again.** With a **quality assessment**, a **donation tier**, a **talent standard**, a **contribution count** or an **export registration**: read the certificate, accept the classification, and depend on the certifier's continued goodwill. **One artefact survives its producer. The others require the producer to keep vouching.** That difference is not cultural and it is not about openness as a virtue — **it is about which party holds a copy.**

**Third, the honest reckoning for the domestic record, stated as a design question rather than a verdict.** This series has spent a month arguing that the ecosystem builds instruments that govern an **artefact's passage** and not an **artefact's production**. Today's ledger entry shows a parallel gap on the disclosure side: **instruments that certify a management system, and no instrument that makes a number checkable by subtraction.** Both gaps have the same shape and the same cheapest remedy — **publish a quantity that can be recomputed by someone who owes you nothing.** OpenAtom's audited series is the single instance in this ecosystem where that has been done, and it happened because a **charity-law annual inspection** compels it. **The lesson is not that Chinese institutions are opaque. It is that disclosure in this ecosystem tracks the regulator's requirement with great fidelity, and where no regulator requires arithmetic, no arithmetic appears.**

**Fourth, what to watch, all of it checkable.** **Whether a regulated buyer publishes a full TCO against the 90 per cent inference saving** — the number that would tell you whether self-hosting Chinese weights is a cost story or a control story, and the likely answer is both. **Whether Z.ai's next GLM release leaves self-hosting unencumbered** — the licence move that would show the vendor understands what an irrecoverable download costs it. **Whether any Chinese AI seller's next statutory filing reconciles to the segment figure it has been quoting** — the cheapest possible test of whether the disclosure problem is a labelling problem or a different problem. **And, unchanged and unfulfilled since October: one merged patch, from one maintainer, employed by someone other than the project's donor.** That is the only number in this series that no filing can produce, no assessment can certify, and every repository can.
